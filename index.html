My Ph.D. research focuses on foundation model interpretability, specifically how they represent information and how insights from explainable AI can inform downstream computer vision tasks and other fields. I am currently a Ph.D. student at the Max Planck Institute for Informatics in Saarbrücken under the supervision of Prof. Dr. Bernt Schiele and Robin Hesse.

I hold an MSc in Autonomous Systems and Robotics and a BSc in Business Information Systems, both from TU Darmstadt. During my studies, I spent a semester on an exchange program at ETH Zurich and the University of Zurich. I was also a scholarship recipient at the Konrad Zuse School of Excellence in Learning and Intelligent Systems (ELIZA).

Publications

DUNE: Disentangling Polysemantic Neurons Without Losing Predictive Performance

Doğukan Bağcı, Bernt Schiele, Simone Schaub-Meyer, Jonas Fischer, Robin Hesse
arXiv 2026

DUNE disentangles foundation model neurons into more monosemantic subunits while preserving their original function by design.

Read DUNE on arXiv

Beyond Accuracy: What Matters in Designing Well-Behaved Image Classification Models?

Robin Hesse*, Doğukan Bağcı*, Bernt Schiele, Simone Schaub-Meyer, Stefan Roth
* Equal contribution
TMLR 2026 Featured Certification

We study nine dimensions of image classification quality, including robustness, calibration, and fairness. We analyze 326 backbone models to see how different scales, training recipes, and model architectures affect these dimensions.
